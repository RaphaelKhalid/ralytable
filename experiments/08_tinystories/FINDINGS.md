# A discrete bottleneck costs 0.63 cross-entropy and 9.9 points of accuracy on real text

This overturns `experiments/06_discrete_core`, which found roughly a third of that
cost on a synthetic corpus and concluded legibility was cheap. It is not, at this
codebook size, on text people actually write.

## Setup

TinyStories (Eldan and Li, 2023), the standard benchmark for how small a language
model can be and still write coherent English. 466,762,900 training tokens, a
custom 8,192-token BPE trained on the corpus itself, stored as a uint16 memmap.

Two configurations, identical in everything except the middle:

- **dense** — three transformer layers, three more, nothing between them
- **codes=512** — the same, with the hidden state snapped to the nearest of 512
  discrete codes at the join

Three seeds each. 10,631 steps, batch 32, context 256, so 87,089,152 tokens per
run. Roughly 3 tokens per parameter, about 0.15x Chinchilla-optimal: both arms
sit at the same point on the same curve, which is what a matched comparison needs.

**Parameters matched to 0.22%**: 29,497,856 dense against 29,563,392 discrete,
verified before training rather than asserted after.

## Result

| | cross-entropy | accuracy | perplexity |
|---|---|---|---|
| dense | **1.6608 ± 0.0025** | **58.77% ± 0.11** | 5.26 |
| codes=512 | **2.2892 ± 0.0789** | **48.85% ± 1.24** | 9.87 |
| gap | **+0.6284** | **-9.92 points** | 1.88x worse |

95% intervals over three seeds. They do not overlap, and are not close to it.

**The dense seeds spread by 0.0025 CE. The gap is 0.63.** That is 250 times the
noise floor, so this is not a seeding accident.

**It is not codebook collapse either.** All three discrete runs ended with
**512 of 512 codes live**. This is the cost of the bottleneck working, not the
cost of it failing. Earlier versions of this code collapsed to 1 live code out of
64; the fixes are in `experiments/06_discrete_core/core.py`.

## Why this differs from experiment 06

Experiment 06 measured about 3 points of accuracy cost and this file's earlier
sibling concluded "legibility is cheap". That corpus was 4,042 word problems
generated by DeepSeek to a fixed template. Formulaic text compresses into a small
alphabet easily; children's stories do not. The result was not wrong, it was a
result about easy data that got read as a result in general.

It also puts this project where the literature already was. Meta's Large Concept
Models lost to their own same-data dense control. The ETH recurrent logic-gate
model reached 4.39 BLEU on WMT'14. Experiment 06 was the outlier.

## What the models actually write

Perplexity is a proxy, so `experiments/09_story_quality` judged the generated text
blind against a threshold committed before any text existed. It failed on both
criteria: grammar 7.00 [6.78, 7.22] against 4.54 [4.27, 4.82], consistency 5.83
[5.58, 6.08] against 3.08 [2.86, 3.30], and dense won 85.4% of 179 pairwise
comparisons.

The failure mode is specific and is not grammar. The discrete model writes
well-formed sentences and loses track of what it is talking about: in one sample
a kite becomes a star of water, then a balloon, then a flag, then a drum, then a
trumpet, inside one paragraph. Syntax is local and survives a nine-bit channel.
Holding an identity across hundreds of tokens does not.

## Limitations

- One codebook size. 512 codes is roughly 9 bits per position; 1024 or 4096 may
  behave differently, and experiment 06 saw larger codebooks do better on both
  axes.
- One architecture family: a transformer with a VQ bottleneck at the midpoint.
  Says nothing about other routes to legibility.
- Deliberately undertrained at 0.15x Chinchilla. Both arms equally, so the
  comparison holds, but absolute quality is not comparable to published
  TinyStories numbers.
- The dense control has no bottleneck at all rather than a matched continuous
  one, so some of the gap may be the bottleneck as a bottleneck rather than as a
  discrete one.
- TinyStories is deliberately simple text. Whether the gap widens or narrows on
  ordinary prose is untested.

## Reproduce

```
python experiments/08_tinystories/run.py --smoke     # whole pipeline, ~3 min
python experiments/08_tinystories/run.py             # 6 runs, ~3.5 h on an 8GB GPU
```

Corpus is cached after the first run. Live dashboard on localhost, W&B mirror on
by default, `--resume` skips finished runs, and a `STOP` file ends the sweep
cleanly at the next checkpoint.
